# Election 2028 — Playtest Improvement Plan

## July 2026 five-run audit

Five seeded general-election playthroughs used distinct campaign identities: balanced persuasion, $5M television saturation, digital-first organizing, field-first organizing, and negative advertising. A browser run also covered the complete eight-step setup and the opening campaign dashboard.

| Strategy | Matchup | Result | Ad buys | Ending cash | Repeated headlines |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Balanced | Newsom–Vance | 209–329 | 12 × $250K | $50.3M | 34% |
| Saturation TV | Pritzker–Youngkin | 214–324 | 12 × $5M | $62.9M | 28% |
| Digital-first | Ocasio-Cortez–Rubio | 205–333 | 12 × $1M | $37.0M | 45% |
| Field-first | Whitmer–DeSantis | 218–320 | 12 × $100K | $49.8M | 28% |
| Negative blitz | Shapiro–Hawley | 228–310 | 12 × $2M | $16.3M | 28% |

The new ad scale works: larger buys create materially larger state movement while retaining diminishing returns. The audit also found that campaign income outpaces meaningful spending, headline repetition remains visible outside debates, and one run won the popular vote while losing the Electoral College without enough explanatory feedback.

## Priority 1 — Make money a real campaign constraint

- Replace passive late-game cash growth with donor fatigue, contribution ceilings, and fundraising opportunity cost.
- Add weekly payroll for staff, offices, security, and sustained media reservations.
- Give saturation buys market-specific inventory limits: the fifth million should be useful, but less efficient in a small state than in a large media market.
- Show marginal impact before purchase and an after-action report comparing cost per persuaded voter across TV and digital.

Success target: a well-run campaign should enter the final month choosing between two or three valuable investments, not holding $40M–$60M after repeated ad buys.

## Priority 2 — Build a campaign “variety director”

- Track recently shown headlines, events, opponent moves, and activity narration; suppress repeats until each pool has cycled.
- Add state-, candidate-, strategy-, and phase-specific variants so the same action feels different in Iowa, Pennsylvania, and California.
- Turn repeated activities into evolving chains: first town hall introduces a voter concern, the second forces a commitment, and the third tests whether the promise survived opposition attacks.
- Keep the new 15-topic debate book as the model: exhaust a content deck before recycling it.

Success target: fewer than 15% duplicate headlines in a 24-week general-election run; no identical event title within six weeks.

## Priority 3 — Explain why the map moved

- Add a weekly “three biggest movers” panel with attributed causes: ads, ticket effects, events, demographics, and polling noise.
- On election night, explain popular-vote/Electoral College splits and identify the tipping-point state.
- Add an optional forecast range instead of presenting one deterministic EV number as certainty.
- Surface opponent spending and organizing on the same state card so reversals feel earned rather than arbitrary.

Success target: after any surprising state result, the player can identify at least two visible contributing causes without opening debug-style statistics.

## Priority 4 — Shorten repeat-run setup

- Add “Quick Rematch” and “Randomize Matchup” from the result screen.
- Preserve the last difficulty, perks, and platform, with a one-click option to edit them.
- Collapse the large VP bench behind filters for shortlist, region, ideology, and map impact.
- Keep the full eight-step first-run experience, but offer a three-step fast setup for returning players.

Success target: a returning player can begin a different matchup in under 30 seconds.

## Priority 5 — Deepen strategic counterplay

- Let opponents contest saturated media markets, rebut negative ads, and force rates upward.
- Create visible synergy and conflict between ads, field offices, coalitions, platform positions, and candidate traits.
- Add debate preparation “answer lanes” that unlock candidate-specific lines rather than only raising a score.
- Introduce campaign staff recommendations that disagree, making strategic identity a recurring decision rather than a static bonus.

## Recommended delivery order

1. Economy rebalance and recurring expenses.
2. Variety director for headlines/events/activity chains.
3. Map attribution and election-night explanations.
4. Quick setup/rematch flow and VP filters.
5. Strategic counters, synergies, and staff doctrine.

Each wave should retain seeded simulation coverage, five-strategy balance runs, desktop and phone smoke tests, and a live Pages asset check.
